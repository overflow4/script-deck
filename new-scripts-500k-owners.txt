COLD CALL SCRIPT — 500K OWNERS

(Different buyer from a solo cleaner. They are not short on leads and they are not broke. They are OUT OF CAPACITY and the owner is the bottleneck. Never open with "you're missing calls" — they have an office manager and it reads as an insult. Open with the specific MOMENTS their setup fails.)

Setter: Hey, is this (name)?

Prospect: Yeah, who's this?

Setter: (name), it's (your name) over at Robinline. You're the owner at (company), right?

Prospect: Yeah, what's this about?

Setter: I'll be straight with you and you can tell me to get lost. I work with cleaning companies doing roughly half a million and up, and the reason I called you specifically is you're past the size where the problem is leads. Can I take thirty seconds and then you decide?

Prospect: Go ahead.

Setter: At your size the phone usually gets handled by someone — office manager, a VA, an answering service. What I keep finding is that the calls still get through fine between nine and five on a Tuesday, and then they leak everywhere else. After six. Saturday morning. The forty minutes she's at lunch. Two calls landing at once. Does that sound familiar or are you covered on all that?

Prospect: I mean, yeah, some of that.

Setter: (Do not pounce. Slow down here.) That's the normal answer. Let me ask you the question that actually matters — when a brand new commercial lead calls at 7pm on a Thursday, what happens to that call right now?

Prospect: Goes to voicemail / the service takes a message / I get a text.

Setter: And realistically, how long before somebody calls them back?

Prospect: Next morning usually.

Setter: Right. So here's my whole pitch. On a residential job, next morning is survivable. On a commercial bid, next morning is fourth in line — the guy who picked up at 7:02 is already walking the building. That's the gap I'd want to look at with you.

Setter: What we run is a receptionist that isn't a person. Picks up on the first ring, any hour, English or Spanish, quotes off YOUR price list — not a generic script — and drops the job straight onto your calendar. Your office manager stops being the bottleneck and starts handling the stuff that actually needs a human.

Prospect: How much is it?

Setter: Fair question, and I'm not going to dodge it — but I'd be guessing if I quoted you before I know your call volume and whether you're mostly resi or commercial. That's exactly what the fifteen minutes is for. Worst case you get a number and a straight answer on whether it's even worth it at your size.

Setter: What does the back half of this week look like for you?
